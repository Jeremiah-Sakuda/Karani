# Personal Statement — Makena Community Fund

I intend to complete a bachelor's degree in civil engineering at Northbay State, with a
focus on water infrastructure, and to return to work for the Tarrenton County water
district, where I have interned for two summers. The goal is specific because the problem
is: Tarrenton's distribution system loses an estimated thirty percent of treated water to
leaks, and the district's own engineering staff is three people, two of whom retire within
five years. My internship supervisor has told me plainly that the district hires from
Northbay's program when it can, and rarely can.

On community involvement: through my school's chapter of the Streamkeepers program I have
done quarterly water quality sampling on Miller Creek since tenth grade — that is eleven
sampling rounds, each logged with the county extension office, and our chapter's data was
cited in the district's culvert replacement application last year. I also taught the
sampling protocol to the incoming cohort this fall, six students.

I will be direct about what I am not including. My family's financial situation is not
something I am willing to lay out in a document that many people will read, beyond saying
that I qualify for the federal Pell Grant, which the committee can verify from my FAFSA, and
that the gap between total aid and Northbay's in-state cost of attendance is $4,100 a year.
I understand this application asks me to articulate need, and I understand a reviewer may
count this section against me. I would rather be counted down for reticence than perform
hardship.

The award covers most of that gap for one year. What it changes is not whether I attend
Northbay — I will attend regardless, on loans if necessary — but whether I graduate with
debt small enough to make a county water district salary a viable choice over the private
consulting offers that pull every Northbay graduate away from exactly the places that need
them. The award does not buy my attendance. It buys where I can afford to work afterward.
