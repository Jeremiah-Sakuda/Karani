# Personal Statement — Makena Community Fund

My goal is an associate degree in electrical engineering technology from Kiptown Technical
College, followed by the utility lineworker certification program that the college runs with
Verandale Municipal Power. I settled on this path in a specific place: the storm season two
years ago, when my neighborhood in East Verandale lost power for nine days and the crews who
restored it were all contracted from out of the region. I spent that week helping my uncle
run extension cords from the one generator on our block, and I decided I wanted to be one of
the people a town does not have to import.

My financial circumstances are these. My mother works as a home health aide at twenty-two
hours a week, which is the maximum her own health allows, and her income covers rent and
part of our food. I work twenty-five hours a week at the Harwick Avenue grocery, and my
paycheck covers the remainder of the household costs plus my transit pass. After those
obligations I can save about forty dollars a month. Tuition for the program is $3,850 a
semester. At my current saving rate, the first semester alone would take eight years to
save for. I am not describing hardship for sympathy; I am showing the arithmetic that makes
this award the difference between enrolling next fall and not enrolling.

For the last three years I have volunteered with the East Verandale Tool Library, a lending
program run out of the community center on Prosper Street. I started as a Saturday desk
volunteer and now maintain the inventory system and train new volunteers — we lend roughly
sixty tools a week to about two hundred registered households. Last spring I organized the
library's first repair clinic, where volunteers fixed forty-one household items in one
afternoon, and we have run it monthly since. The center's coordinator, Ms. Adisa, can
confirm the dates and numbers.

What this award changes is simple and mechanical. It covers first-year tuition, which means
I can cut my grocery hours to fifteen a week, which is what makes attending daytime labs
possible at all — the program does not offer an evening section. Without the award I would
not attend more slowly; I would not attend, because the labs and my current work schedule
cannot coexist. With it, I enroll in the fall, keep my transit pass, and keep my Saturday
shift at the tool library, which I have no intention of giving up.
